## Overview

Most of the time you are not sitting in front of the portal. You are on a job, with a
customer, or driving. When something genuinely needs you, waiting until you next open the
app is too late.

DPF can reach you by email when a decision is yours to make. This page explains what gets
sent, what does not, and why you still have to open the app to decide.

## What gets sent to you

Only things that need **you**, personally, and soon:

- a bill or expense to approve
- a message to a customer waiting for your sign-off
- a filing that needs approving
- a booking that needs your decision
- a customer waiting for a first reply
- something your customers rely on that has stopped working

## What never gets sent

**Technical problems do not reach you.** If a service is unhealthy, a scheduled job failed,
or a connection needs reconnecting, that is the platform's job to fix and your technical
custodian's job to oversee. Emailing you about a container at 2am is exactly the noise this
is designed to remove.

**Low-urgency items are grouped.** Anything that can sensibly wait arrives in your weekly
digest instead of interrupting you one at a time.

**Nothing is sent if you have not set up a channel.** Email reaches you only once an
address is set up and verified.

## What the message says — and why it says so little

The email tells you *what kind* of thing needs you and gives you a link. It does not include
the supplier, the amount, the customer's message, or what an AI coworker is proposing.

This is deliberate. An email is easy to fake and easy to intercept, and some of that content
is written by people outside your business. If you could approve things based on what an
email said, then whoever wrote that content could influence your decision. So the message
gets you *to* the decision; the decision itself always happens in the app, where you can see
the real thing next to what your coworker thinks about it.

## Why you still have to sign in

Tapping the link takes you to the sign-in screen, and after signing in you land on the exact
item — not a list you have to search.

There is deliberately **no approve button in the email**. Anything involving money leaving
your business, anything going out publicly, and anything that cannot be undone always
requires you to be signed in. A one-tap approval in an email is a one-tap approval for
anyone who gets hold of that email.

If a link says it has expired, nothing is wrong — the item is probably still waiting for you.
Open the app and it will be there.

## Turning it off or changing it

Channels are per-person and can be turned off. Urgency thresholds are configurable, so you
can choose to be reached only for urgent things.
