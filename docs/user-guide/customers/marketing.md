## Use This Guide For

Use **Customer → Marketing** to decide what demand to improve, turn that decision
into a campaign, review evidence and draft assets, and deliberately publish
through a connected channel. Marketing is not a general content generator: it is
an operator-owned path from business context to measurable customer acquisition.

The Marketing tab appears only for people with the marketing capability.
Organization setup and a Storefront archetype provide the business context that
keeps recommendations, examples, and campaign language relevant.

## Begin With One Decision

The first panel asks one owner-level question and recommends one next step. For a
restaurant, that might be whether to fill slow nights, grow private dining,
promote catering, or launch a seasonal menu. For another archetype, the wording
and suggested motion change with that business.

1. Read the question and the metric the recommendation is intended to move.
2. Decide whether the recommended step addresses the current constraint.
3. Choose the call to action or start a **Marketing Strategist** review.
4. Keep the outcome specific enough to measure: an audience, offer, channel,
   proof point, time window, and desired response.

Avoid opening every advanced panel before making that first decision. The page
uses progressive disclosure so strategy machinery does not obscure the
operator's immediate choice.

## Review Strategy, Assumptions, and Proof

Open **Strategy, assumptions & proof** when the recommendation needs scrutiny.
Review:

- market and geographic focus;
- the first buyer group or ideal-customer profile;
- route to market and primary channels;
- the latest saved strategist recommendation;
- channels deliberately skipped for now;
- the KPI stack used to evaluate the work;
- proof assets that make the offer credible;
- stale assumptions and the next review date.

Use **View current assumptions** for the detailed strategy surface. Ask the
strategist to challenge weak or generic assumptions, make the plan market-ready,
or prepare a first campaign. A strategist response should save durable
recommendations where the UI can show them; a useful idea that exists only in a
chat is not yet an operating plan.

## Build a Campaign Before Building Assets

Open **Campaigns** after the acquisition decision is clear. A campaign brief
should establish:

- objective and measurable outcome;
- audience;
- channel mix;
- offer and proof;
- KPI set;
- asset tasks and their due window;
- approval posture and next work.

Campaign briefs precede individual assets. This order prevents a polished post
or email from becoming the de facto strategy. Asset tasks are the concrete work
items that can become approval-gated drafts.

When no campaign exists, return to the strategist workspace rather than creating
unrelated assets. When an asset is incomplete, revise the task or proof before
moving it into review.

## Review and Publish

The **Review & publish queue** appears after there is archetype-fit campaign work
to review. A fresh workspace deliberately shows an unlock message instead of
empty operational queues.

Before publishing:

1. Confirm the campaign and asset fit the current business archetype.
2. Read the complete draft, audience, destination, and channel.
3. Check proof, links, timing, consent, and contact restrictions.
4. Confirm the correct channel connection is available.
5. Use the channel's explicit publish/send action and confirmation.
6. Preserve the approval and resulting channel evidence.

Publishing is an external side effect. Approval of a draft and transmission to a
channel are separate decisions; neither should be inferred from the existence of
generated content.

## Quarantined or Imported Content

If saved material uses language from a different business type—for example,
software-platform copy in a restaurant workspace—the platform flags it as
imported/test content and blocks it from publishing.

Do not edit only the headline to clear the symptom. Replace or reset the
off-archetype campaign, its asset tasks, and any dependent draft so audience,
offer, proof, and measurement all describe the actual business.

## Understand the Two Funnels

- **Marketing Funnel** (`/customer/marketing/funnel`) explains acquisition
  sources and how marketing interactions convert into CRM pipeline.
- **Sales Funnel** (`/customer/funnel`) combines the last 30 days of Storefront
  interactions with engagement, opportunity, and won-work stages.

Use Marketing Funnel to decide where acquisition work is weak. Use Sales Funnel
to identify the weakest handoff from interest to engagement, engagement to
opportunity, or opportunity to won work. A low conversion rate is a diagnostic
signal; inspect the underlying records before changing the campaign.

## Treat Automation as a Proposal

**Automation** shows candidate workflows, triggers, intended actions, and
approval posture. The surface is read-only: a candidate does not publish, send,
schedule, or mutate CRM data.

Before later enabling an automation, define:

- the exact trigger and its evidence source;
- the audience and action;
- approval, pause, and cancellation rules;
- **Do not contact** and channel-consent enforcement;
- audit events and a recovery path;
- the metric that determines whether the automation is helping.

Keep human review at the boundary where work leaves DPF or changes a customer
record.

## A Practical Operating Rhythm

### Before launch

- Confirm business archetype, offer, audience, proof, and channel connection.
- Save the campaign brief and measurable outcome.
- Review every asset and reject imported/test artifacts.

### During the campaign

- Work the approval queue.
- Watch inbound replies and route qualified interest into Engagements.
- Compare channel activity with Storefront and CRM evidence.
- Record decisions that change audience, offer, proof, or spend.

### After the review window

- Compare the intended KPI stack with observed results.
- Inspect the weakest funnel handoff.
- Keep, revise, or stop the campaign explicitly.
- Turn repeatable gaps into backlog work instead of leaving them in analysis.

## What To Watch

- campaign ideas generated without checking the current funnel constraint;
- customer-acquisition analysis drifting away from Storefront reality;
- content that sounds plausible but does not fit the selected archetype;
- publishing before proof, consent, audience, or channel is confirmed;
- automation candidates being mistaken for enabled workflows;
- marketing changes that should instead be solved in the offer, customer
  follow-up, product, or fulfilment operation.
