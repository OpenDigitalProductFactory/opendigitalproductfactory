## Keep Doing The Work You Get Paid For

Most owners are still doing the job. The plumber still takes the call-outs. The salon owner still cuts hair. The clinic owner still chases missing forms. The venue owner still runs down deposits.

Then, after hours, comes the work around the work: quoting, booking, following up, ordering stock, drafting the reminder, checking who is covered tomorrow.

That second pile is what this platform takes on.

> Keep doing the work customers pay you for. Give the work around the work to governed AI coworkers.

"Governed" is the important word. Your AI coworkers draft, chase, prepare, and flag. They do not send, spend, or publish on their own. You approve anything that reaches a customer or moves money, and every action is logged.

You do not need to be technical to run this. Nothing here asks you to write code.

## It Starts With Your Kind Of Business

The first question is not "which module do you want". It is **what kind of business do you run**.

Pick that, and the platform arrives already shaped: the right words, the right forms, the right daily board, and coworkers that know the job. A plumbing business gets jobs, trucks, parts, and techs. A dental practice gets appointments, patients, forms, and practitioners. A pet boarding business gets stays, pets, and care notes.

There are **103 business types across 23 categories** to start from:

| If you run… | Categories that cover it |
|---|---|
| A trade or a van | Trades and maintenance · Automotive services · Moving and logistics |
| Appointments and a chair | Beauty and personal care · Healthcare and wellness · Pet services |
| A room full of people | Fitness and recreation · Education and training · Live events and venues |
| A shop or stock | Retail and goods · Food and hospitality |
| Things customers leave in your care | Fabric care services · Warehousing and fulfilment |
| Expertise you sell | Professional services · Media production · Software platform |
| Property or assets | HOA and property management · Asset rental · Real estate and construction |
| A mission or a community | Nonprofit and community · Public sector and civic |
| Money and trust | Banking and financial services · Security services |

Do not worry about finding a perfect match. Pick the closest one — you can refine the details afterwards, and the vocabulary follows your business, not the other way round.

The full list, and what each category changes, is in [Market Archetypes And Coworkers](../market-archetypes.md).

## What You Get When You Pick One

- **A customer-facing portal** — your public front door, with the right sections, forms, and either a booking flow or an enquiry flow depending on how your business actually takes work.
- **A daily board** — the first screen your team sees, showing the exceptions that need a person: the quote nobody chased, the shift nobody covered, the stock about to run out.
- **Your own words** — jobs, patients, orders, volunteers, residents, guests, members. Whatever your business calls things.
- **Coworkers who know the job** — a setup helper, a storefront and marketing helper, and helpers for customers, money, people, and compliance.
- **A marketing posture that matches** — offers, proof points, and campaign ideas that suit your trade instead of generic filler.

## Your First Hour

1. **Set up the business.** Create the owner account and describe the business: where you are, who you serve, and how careful you need to be with data. Walk through it in [Setup And First Login](setup-and-first-login.md).
2. **Choose your business type.** This is the decision everything else inherits from.
3. **Open your portal and check the words.** If it calls your customers the wrong thing, fix that first — see [Storefront](../storefront/index.md).
4. **Meet your coworker.** Press the button in the bottom-right of any screen and ask it what the page is for. Details in [AI Coworker](ai-coworker.md).
5. **Add your people.** Give each person the access that matches their job — see [Roles & Access](roles-and-access.md).

## Finding Your Way Around

Down the left side is a rail, grouped by the kind of work rather than by screen.

| Group | What lives there | Who uses it most |
|---|---|---|
| **Workspace** | Your queue, recent items, and AI-suggested next steps | Everyone, every day |
| **Business** | Customers, people, money, compliance, and your public portal | Owners and managers |
| **Knowledge** | Shared reference, documents, and this guide | Everyone |
| **Products** | Portfolio, backlog, and architecture | Businesses that build a product |
| **Delivery** | Build and ship work from one place | Businesses that build a product |
| **Platform** | AI coworker settings and platform administration | Whoever administers the install |

**If that feels like too much, switch the rail to Simple.** It hides everything except the day-to-day business work. The full rail is always one toggle away, so you cannot get stranded.

You only ever see what your role allows and what your business type actually turned on. An empty group is not a fault — it means that part does not apply to you.

The three groups most owners live in are **Workspace**, **Business**, and **Knowledge**. If you run a business rather than build software, you may never need Products or Delivery at all.

## Your AI Coworker

Every screen has a coworker available from the button in the bottom-right corner.

It knows which page you are on, what kind of business this is, and what it is allowed to do there. Ask it in plain language — "who hasn't been chased this week", "what's short for tomorrow", "draft a reply to this".

Your standing coworker is a **COO**, and you can give it a name of your choosing. Naming it changes nothing about what it is allowed to do. Some installs also let you talk to it instead of typing.

See [AI Coworker](ai-coworker.md) for what it can and cannot do on your behalf.

## When You Want Something Changed

If the platform does not do something your business needs, you do not file a ticket and wait.

**Build Studio** is the governed way to ask for a change and watch it get made, reviewed, and shipped. It is guided, and it does not require you to write anything technical. See [Build Studio](../build-studio/index.md).

If you are a developer working on the platform's source code, that is a different job with a different setup — see [Contributing & Dev Setup](../contributing/index.md).
