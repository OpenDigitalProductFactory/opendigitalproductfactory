## Rule

Scrum rests on **empiricism** — knowledge comes from experience and decisions from observation. It is upheld by three pillars:

- **Transparency** — the process and the work must be **visible** to those performing and receiving the work.
- **Inspection** — Scrum artifacts and progress toward goals are **examined frequently** to detect variance.
- **Adaptation** — when deviation is detected, adjust the process or product "as soon as possible" to minimize it.

> Sources: 2020 Scrum Guide (CC-BY-SA). SAFe reinforces this via build-incrementally-with-fast-learning-cycles and objective milestones (licensed, paraphrased).

## Why

The pillars are why the events exist: each [[professions/scrum-master/the-five-scrum-events]] is a structured moment to inspect and adapt against transparent information. Remove any pillar and the empirical loop breaks — opaque work cannot be inspected, and inspection without adaptation is theater.

## How To Apply

1. **Make work visible** — shared backlog, board, and definition of done.
2. **Inspect on cadence** — use the events, not ad-hoc check-ins.
3. **Adapt immediately** — turn inspection findings into changes, including to the team's own process via [[professions/scrum-master/sprint-retrospective-continuous-improvement]].
