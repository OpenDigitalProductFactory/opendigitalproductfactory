## Heuristic

**Good Distribution Practice (GDP)** governs the storage and distribution of medicinal products; the same discipline applies to food and other perishables. The cold chain "must be maintained" and continuously monitored.

For a cold or temperature-controlled operator:

- **Monitor continuously, not spot-check.** Every temperature-controlled zone logs continuously; the record is the evidence a client and an auditor rely on.
- **Treat an excursion as an event.** A temperature deviation is recorded, investigated, and the affected product quarantined pending a disposition decision — not silently ignored because it "came back in range."
- **Prove integrity at handover.** The temperature record travels with the goods so the next party can see the chain was unbroken; an unmonitored gap is a broken chain even if the product looks fine.

## Why it matters

Cold-chain product that has been out of range may be unsafe or unsaleable, and the operator cannot tell by looking. Continuous monitoring plus excursion handling is what lets the operator hand over provable integrity — the whole reason a client pays for temperature-controlled storage over ambient.
