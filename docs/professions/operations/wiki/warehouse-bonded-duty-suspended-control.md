## Heuristic

A **bonded warehouse** stores imported goods with **duty and import VAT suspended** until the goods are released. The operator holds a customs bond and is accountable to the customs authority for the goods while they are under bond.

For a bonded operator:

- **Keep the bonded stock auditable and separate.** Customs must be able to reconcile receipts against releases at any time; bonded and duty-paid stock are not commingled.
- **Account for duty at the right trigger.** Duty and import VAT fall due when goods are **released for home use**; re-export or onward bonded movement may keep the suspension intact — the release type sets the liability.
- **Never release without the paperwork.** A physical release ahead of the customs entry is an unaccounted movement against the bond, which is the operator's liability, not the client's.

## Why it matters

The bond makes the operator answerable for the duty on every item under it. A release recorded late or against the wrong regime turns a routine despatch into a customs discrepancy the operator owns. Bonded control is the discipline that lets a client defer duty legitimately without exposing the warehouse.
