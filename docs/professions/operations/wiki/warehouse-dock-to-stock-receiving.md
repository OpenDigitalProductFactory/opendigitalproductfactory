## Heuristic

A warehouse management system tracks goods from **receiving through put-away, storage, picking, and despatch**. The load-bearing inbound metric is **dock-to-stock**: the elapsed time from a receipt arriving on the dock to it being put away and available to pick.

For the receiving team:

- **Check every receipt against the advance ship notice (ASN)** before it is accepted — quantity, condition, and lot/expiry — so a discrepancy is caught at the door, not at the pick face.
- **Put away to a known location** and record it; stock in an unrecorded location is not pickable stock, even though it is physically in the building.
- **Work to the dock-to-stock target** (commonly under 48 hours, best-in-class under 24) — a receipt sitting on the dock is capacity that cannot be sold and orders that cannot be filled.

## Why it matters

Every downstream pick depends on the inbound being correct: if a receipt is mis-counted or put away wrong, the error surfaces as a short-ship or a wrong-item despatch to the client's own customer. Dock-to-stock and inventory accuracy are the two numbers a 3PL is actually managed to.
