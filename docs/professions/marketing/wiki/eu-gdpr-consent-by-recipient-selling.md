## Heuristic

Marketing consent keys off **where the recipient is**, not where the business operates. Under the GDPR (Article 7, conditions for consent), consent must be **demonstrable**, given in **clear and plain language**, and **as easy to withdraw as to give**. So a business operating only in the US that markets to EU contacts must still meet the EU opt-in standard for those contacts.

For marketing and CRM into the EU:

- Use **opt-in** (affirmative, unbundled consent) for EU recipients — not a pre-ticked box and not opt-out.
- Keep a **record of consent** (who, when, what they were told) so it is demonstrable.
- Make **withdrawal one step**, and honor it across every channel.
- Segment the list by recipient jurisdiction so EU contacts get the EU rule even when the rest of the list is governed by a lighter opt-out regime (e.g. US CAN-SPAM).

## Why it matters

Consent obligations cross borders with the customer, not the company. A US business selling into the EU that applies only US opt-out rules is non-compliant for its EU recipients — and "we're based in the US" is not a defense.
