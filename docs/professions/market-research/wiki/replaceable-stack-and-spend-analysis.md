## What This Covers

Owner-facing competitive intelligence: given a prospect or market segment, work
out what software they likely run, what it costs, and which of those tools the
platform consolidates — so the sales pitch ("we replace N of your tools") is sized
from evidence, not assertion.

## How To Analyse a Prospect's Stack

1. **Ground in the CRM first.** Read the named opportunity/account (read-only) for
   the industry, size, and any recorded systems. The brief is for a specific deal,
   not a generic segment.
2. **Enumerate the likely stack by category.** Group candidate tools —
   accounting/payroll, CRM, MSP backup/patching, ticketing, productivity — and
   list the common vendors in each for a business of that shape.
3. **Size the spend in ranges.** Pull public per-seat / per-month pricing and give
   bands, never invented precision. A range with a source beats a single number
   without one.
4. **Flag the replaceable subset.** Mark which categories the platform covers and
   estimate the consolidated spend the prospect could recover.
5. **Note switching considerations.** Lock-in, data migration, and integration
   risk sit alongside the upside — a one-sided brief is not decision-useful.

## The Hard Rule — Cite Every Number

Every figure and claim cites a source actually retrieved with the research tools.
When a figure cannot be found, say so and give a clearly-labelled estimate range.
An unsourced number presented as fact is a fabrication, and fabrication is the one
failure this role exists to avoid — a plausible but invented "$40/seat" is worse
than an honest "public pricing not found; comparable tools run ~$25–60/seat."

## Output

A brief attached to the opportunity: the replaceable-tool landscape, typical spend
with citations, the consolidation estimate, and switching considerations — enough
for the owner to size and open the conversation with confidence.
