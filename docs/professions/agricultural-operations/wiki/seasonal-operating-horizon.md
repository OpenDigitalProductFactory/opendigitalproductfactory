## Heuristic

Maintain one horizon with four bands: 0–14 days, 15–90 days, the coming season, and the next one to five years. For each field, pasture, herd group, animal, machine, implement, material, vendor, obligation, and sale decision, record the preferred window, latest-safe date, dependencies, evidence date, approval owner, and fallback.

A deadline is not ready when any prerequisite is unresolved. Hay cutting, for example, can depend on crop stage, a multi-day forecast, cutter and baler readiness, labor, twine or wrap, storage, a custom operator, and transport. The calendar should surface the earliest dependency date, not merely the hoped-for cutting day.

Long-range outlooks are scenario inputs rather than field-level promises. Pair them with current local observations and shorter-range forecasts as the window approaches. State the issue time and confidence; replace stale evidence rather than silently carrying it forward.

## Human authority

The plan may propose and remind. The owner confirms production choices and spend; qualified professionals remain authoritative for veterinary, agronomic, chemical, legal, tax, and mechanical decisions.
