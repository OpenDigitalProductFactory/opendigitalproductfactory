## Record pattern

Keep an individual record whenever identity, lineage, health, treatment, withdrawal, movement, breeding, ownership, workload, or service history matters. Aggregate those records into herd, breeding group, pasture, pen, or working team so the owner can see what is due and where a gap affects many animals.

For cattle, retain identity, birth/acquisition, group and location history, breeding and calving facts, observed condition, veterinarian-authored health plan, treatments and withdrawal intervals, movements, and sale/disposition records. Requirements vary by species, movement, and jurisdiction; the current official rule and veterinarian or animal-health authority must be checked before movement.

Working horses are animals with a job, not employees and not generic equipment. Their record includes vaccinations and veterinarian-authored care, dental and hoof/farrier schedule, injuries and restrictions, workload and rest, tack or shoeing needs, handler availability, and the human who must be present for an appointment.

## Authority boundary

The coworker may identify missing records, calculate due dates from a veterinarian-authored plan, arrange an internal reminder, and prepare questions. It must not diagnose, select a drug or vaccine, invent a schedule, alter a withdrawal interval, or decide fitness for work or transport.
