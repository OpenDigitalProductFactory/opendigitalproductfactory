## Why it's cited

The canonical anchor for `[[principles/elicit-tacit-knowledge]]`. It is the source of the claim DPF inherits: the bottleneck in a knowledge platform is acquisition (getting tacit expertise into the system), not retrieval. Decades later this is still the under-served half of "second brain" practice — tooling obsesses over recall while the real loss is knowledge that never left the human's head.

## Key claims

- The rate-limiting step in knowledge-system construction is eliciting the expert's tacit knowledge, not encoding or querying it.
- Tacit expertise is applied fluently but articulated poorly; it must be actively drawn out, not volunteered.
- Structured, persistent interviewing outperforms passive capture for surfacing what the expert knows.
