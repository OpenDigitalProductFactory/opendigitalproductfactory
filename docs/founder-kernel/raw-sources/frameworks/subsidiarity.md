abstract: |
  Subsidiarity is the organizing principle that a matter should be handled by
  the smallest, lowest, or least-centralized competent authority — and that a
  higher or more central authority should act only in a subsidiary (supporting,
  advisory) role, never displacing the local authority's decision unless the
  local level genuinely cannot act. Originating in Catholic social teaching
  (Quadragesimo Anno, 1931) and later load-bearing in European Union law (Treaty
  on European Union, 1992) and federal governance generally, it is the canonical
  statement of scoped, non-inheriting authority: central doctrine supports but
  does not override local competence. Abstract + locator per

## Why it's cited

The anchor for `[[principles/decisions-belong-to-their-scope]]`. DPF's non-inherit boundary between its decision scopes — a customer organization's WWWD is authoritative for its own business; the platform's WWMD doctrine is advisory there, never binding — is subsidiarity applied to a software platform. The higher scope (founder/platform) supports but does not override the more local competent authority (the organization, or the professional in their craft).

## Key claims

- A decision belongs at the most local level competent to make it.
- Higher or more central authority is subsidiary — it supports and advises, it does not override a competent local decision.
- Central authority acts directly only when the local level genuinely cannot act; otherwise its role is enabling, not displacing.
