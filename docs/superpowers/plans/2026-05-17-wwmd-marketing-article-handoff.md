## 1. Working Thesis

The article should explain WWMD as the seed of a broader product pattern:

> A useful AI business system should not only answer questions. It should learn how its leaders decide, preserve constructive conflict, require evidence, and earn autonomy through accountable outcomes.

WWMD starts with "What Would Mark Do" for the DPF platform. WWWD generalizes the pattern into "What Would We Do" for each organization once that organization has enough leader doctrine, decisions, and rationale.

## 3. Narrative Arc

1. Start with the human moment: people asked "what would Mark do?" after seeing a scoped GPT built from Mark's product and platform thinking.
2. Acknowledge the humility and risk: it is flattering, but cloning a person is not the goal.
3. Reframe the problem: organizations do not need a personality bot; they need a governed way to preserve how leaders make decisions.
4. Introduce WWMD as the seed kernel for DPF and WWWD as the tenant/business pattern.
5. Explain why ambiguity, debate, and approval gates matter more than generic Q&A.
6. Introduce constructive conflict: disagreement is a real team pattern, and AI coworkers should preserve it rather than hide it.
7. Introduce evidence-based decisioning: facts, interpretations, preferences, assumptions, and unknowns must stay separate.
8. Explain the confidence doctrine: confidence is earned in drops and lost in buckets.
9. Close with the product direction: start in Build Studio where real work, gates, evidence, and human decisions already exist.

## 4. Key Points to Include

- WWMD is not "AI pretends to be Mark."
- WWMD is a governed decision perspective profile.
- For the current DPF portal instance, WWMD and WWWD overlap because the business/product is the portal itself.
- For future customers, WWWD must be their own profile, trained from their leadership material and decisions.
- Autonomy should grow only as evidence-backed decisions prove reliable.
- Human corrections should reduce confidence quickly.
- Constructive conflict is a feature, not a bug.
- Evidence turns debate from opinion exchange into trustworthy decision support.
- Escalated decisions should capture criteria and rationale so future decisions improve.
- The standalone "Ask WWMD" experience is valuable, but the first product win is a Build Studio autonomy gate.

## 7. Suggested Structure

### Hook

People asking "what would Mark do?" is flattering. But the more interesting question is: how could a company teach its AI coworkers what *we* would do when work gets ambiguous?

### Core Argument

Most AI systems treat memory as recall. WWMD treats memory as decision material: sources, rationale, corrections, outcomes, and confidence.

### Product Pattern

Decision Perspective Profiles:

- who owns the perspective
- where it applies
- what sources it uses
- how confidence changes
- when it recommends, arbitrates, or escalates

### Constructive Conflict

Real teams do not always agree. AI coworkers should be allowed to disagree, surface evidence, and escalate stalemates to the accountable human.

### Evidence and Trust

A recommendation should say what is fact, what is interpretation, what is preference, and what is unknown. That is how trust grows.

### Close

Full autonomy is not one leap. It is earned through many accountable decisions, with evidence, correction, and confidence that can go down as well as up.

## 8. Call to Action

Invite readers to think about the question:

> If your AI coworkers had to answer "what would we do here?", what sources, decisions, and leadership principles would they need to earn your trust?

## 9. Drafting Instructions for the Marketing Strategist

When drafting:

1. Write for LinkedIn first, roughly 900-1,300 words.
2. Lead with humility and the origin story.
3. Keep the article accessible to non-engineering executives, but include enough architecture that product/AI leaders see the seriousness.
4. Avoid overselling autonomy. Emphasize accountability, evidence, and earned confidence.
5. End with a question that invites discussion rather than a sales pitch.
